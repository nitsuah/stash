# Search strategy, networking and outreach

## 1. Define the target before searching

Write a one-paragraph target the candidate can test listings against:

> *Role family* (e.g. platform / DevOps engineering) at *level* (senior IC) in *industries or problem spaces* (health, fintech), *location/remote* (remote US or DC hybrid), at or above *pay floor*, starting *when*, with *hard constraints* (no sponsorship needed / need sponsorship, ≤10% travel).

- Use [O*NET OnLine](https://www.onetonline.org/) or [My Next Move](https://www.mynextmove.org/) to find adjacent titles for the same work. Employers name the same job five ways.
- Check outlook and pay in the [BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/) and the [CareerOneStop Salary Finder](https://www.careeronestop.org/Toolkit/Wages/find-salary.aspx) before setting the floor.
- Pick 2–3 role families at most. More dilutes tailoring.

## 2. Spread across channels

Referrals and direct relationships convert far better than cold applications; job boards are for discovery, not the whole plan. A balanced week:

| Channel | Share of effort | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Targeted applications (boards, company sites) | ~40% | Use ats-fill Search across sources, then verify on the employer's site |
| Networking, referrals, informational chats | ~40% | Highest conversion; start before you need it |
| Skills, portfolio, public work | ~10% | Close the most common gap you see in JDs |
| Admin: tracking, follow-ups, analytics | ~10% | ats-fill pipeline + weekly analytics review |

Also consider: recruiters who specialize in the field, alumni networks, professional associations, local [American Job Centers](https://www.careeronestop.org/LocalHelp/service-locator.aspx), and [USAJOBS](https://www.usajobs.gov/) for US federal roles (federal resumes are longer and must show how each requirement is met).

## 3. Triage each listing in five minutes

1. Read the whole JD, not just the title and bullets.
2. Mark each requirement **must-have**, **strong signal**, **learnable**, or **preference**. Many "requirements" are wish lists; roughly 60–70% coverage of real must-haves is usually enough to apply.
3. State the job's mission in one sentence: what problem are they paying to solve?
4. Check hard constraints: location, authorization, pay range, schedule, clearance, licensing.
5. Note process signals: posting age, pay transparency, realistic scope for the level, reviews from current employees.
6. Record the verdict and two-line rationale in the ats-fill pipeline **before** applying.

Skip or deprioritize: roles that fail a hard constraint, reposted-for-months listings with no recruiter contact, and anything showing scam signals ([FTC: job scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/job-scams)).

## 4. Networking that doesn't feel transactional

Goal: learn, build relationships, and become a known quantity. Referrals follow.

**Who to contact**: former colleagues and managers first, then alumni, people in the target role 1–2 levels above, hiring managers at target companies, community or meetup organizers.

**Informational interview** ([CareerOneStop guide](https://www.careeronestop.org/JobSearch/Network/informational-interviews.aspx)): 20 minutes, the candidate's agenda, the other person's time.

Good questions:
- What does strong performance look like in your role after a year?
- Which skills matter most day to day, and which are overrated in job posts?
- What separates people who get hired here from those who don't?
- How does the team actually decide what to work on?
- If you were starting in this field now, what would you learn first?
- Who else would you suggest I talk to?

**After**: thank-you within 24 hours mentioning one specific thing learned; report back later if you acted on their advice. Ask about referrals only once there's rapport and a specific role.

## 5. Outreach templates (adapt; keep each under ~120 words)

**Warm contact, asking to learn**

> Hi Sam, hope things are good at Northwind. I'm exploring senior platform roles and I'd value 20 minutes of your perspective on how your team works and what it looks for. Any chance next Tue or Wed afternoon works? Happy to work around you. Thanks either way!

**Cold, to someone in the target role**

> Hi Priya, I read your post on migrating Northwind's CI to ephemeral runners. The cost breakdown was really useful. I'm a platform engineer exploring teams that care about developer experience, and I'd love to hear how you approached the rollout. Would you be open to a 20-minute call in the next couple of weeks?

**Referral request (after rapport, specific role)**

> Thanks again for the chat last month. Northwind just posted a Senior Platform Engineer role [link] that maps closely to the CI and reliability work we discussed: I led a similar migration that cut build times 40%. Would you be comfortable referring me, or pointing me to the hiring manager? I've attached my resume and a 3-line summary you can forward.

**Follow-up after no reply (once, ~1 week later)**

> Hi Priya, just floating this back up in case it got buried. Totally understand if now's busy.

## 6. Weekly cadence and metrics

- Set **input** goals the candidate controls: e.g. 5–8 tailored applications, 3–5 networking conversations, 1 skill or portfolio improvement per week.
- Track outcomes in the pipeline and review ats-fill Analytics weekly. Rough health signals: if response rate stays under ~10% after 20+ applications, revisit targeting and resume; if screens happen but no onsites, work on interview stories; if onsites but no offers, run mock interviews and ask for feedback.
- Protect energy: time-box the search, schedule breaks, and celebrate inputs, not just outcomes.
