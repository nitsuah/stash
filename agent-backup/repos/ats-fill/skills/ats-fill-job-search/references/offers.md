# Offers, negotiation and setbacks

This is general guidance, not legal, tax or financial advice. For contracts, equity terms, immigration or non-competes, the candidate should consult a qualified professional.

## Before an offer exists

- **Research the range** for the role, level and location: [CareerOneStop Salary Finder](https://www.careeronestop.org/Toolkit/Wages/find-salary.aspx), [BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics](https://www.bls.gov/oes/), posted ranges in similar listings (ats-fill Search shows pay where boards publish it), and for tech, [Levels.fyi](https://www.levels.fyi/).
- **Expectation questions early**: give a researched range anchored at or slightly above the target, or defer: "I'm focused on fit first; I'm confident we can land on something fair for the scope. What's the budgeted range?"
- **Salary history**: several US states and cities prohibit employers from asking it. The candidate can decline politely and redirect to expectations.

## When the offer arrives

1. **Thank them and show enthusiasm**. Don't accept or decline on the spot. Ask for the details in writing and a reasonable decision window (commonly a few days to a week).
2. **Understand the full package**: base, bonus (target and how it's actually paid), equity (type, vesting, cliff, refreshers), sign-on, benefits and premiums, retirement match, PTO, remote/hybrid terms, start date, title and level, relocation, and any clawbacks.
3. **Compare like with like**: annualize everything; value equity conservatively; account for benefit costs and commute.

## Negotiating

Negotiating a first offer is normal and rarely costs an offer when done respectfully. See [CareerOneStop: Negotiate your salary](https://www.careeronestop.org/JobSearch/Interview/negotiate-your-salary.aspx) and Patrick McKenzie's [Salary Negotiation](https://www.kalzumeus.com/2012/01/23/salary-negotiation/).

- **Lead with enthusiasm, then the ask**, backed by market data, scope, or a competing offer (only a real one).
- **Ask for a specific number**, slightly above target, not a range.
- **Negotiate the package**: if base is capped, ask about sign-on, equity, level/title, start date, remote flexibility, extra PTO, or a 6-month compensation review in writing.
- **Make it easy to say yes**: "If you can get to $X base, I'm ready to sign this week."
- **Pause after asking.** Silence is fine.
- **Get the final terms in writing** before resigning anywhere.

Script:

> Thank you, I'm really excited about this role and the team. Based on the scope we discussed and market data for senior platform roles in this area, I was expecting a base closer to $185,000. If we can get there, I'm ready to accept.

**Multiple processes**: tell other companies you have an offer and a deadline; ask if they can accelerate. Never invent an offer.

## Deciding

Score each option on what matters to the candidate: the work itself, manager and team, growth, compensation, stability, flexibility, commute, values. Talk to a future peer if possible. Decline other offers promptly and graciously; paths cross again.

## Rejections and silence

- Silence after applying is the norm, not a judgment. Follow up once after an interview if the timeline passes.
- After a rejection post-interview: thank them, ask once for brief feedback, and ask to be considered for future roles. Some will reply.
- Log the outcome in ats-fill with the stage reached. Patterns across 15–20 applications are signal; a single "no" is noise.
- Look after energy and finances: keep a routine, stay connected to people, and check [unemployment benefits](https://www.careeronestop.org/LocalHelp/UnemploymentBenefits/unemployment-benefits.aspx) or local [American Job Center](https://www.careeronestop.org/LocalHelp/service-locator.aspx) services if eligible.
