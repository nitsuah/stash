# Income → FIRE date

fire projects in **today's dollars**, so keep everything in real terms.

## Two levers from one stream

1. **Spend it:** it covers expenses forever, so the FIRE number falls.
   `FIRE number reduction = annual income ÷ SWR`. At a 4% SWR, every
   **$100 a month** of durable income is **$30,000** less you need invested.
2. **Save it:** it raises the savings rate, so the portfolio grows faster.
   Use **Projections → Growth Settings → Annual Job Savings** (add the stream's
   annual net) and read the new FIRE age.

Only count income as "durable" (lever 1) if it plausibly survives retirement
(cash yield, dividends, a mature digital catalog). Reselling and gig work stop
when you do, so model those as lever 2 only.

## Quick table (4% SWR)

| Net per month | Per year | FIRE number ↓ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $50 | $600 | $15,000 |
| $100 | $1,200 | $30,000 |
| $250 | $3,000 | $75,000 |
| $500 | $6,000 | $150,000 |
| $1,000 | $12,000 | $300,000 |

At a 3.5% SWR, multiply the last column by about 1.14. At 3%, multiply by about 1.33.

## Years sooner (rule of thumb)

Years saved ≈ FIRE-number reduction ÷ (annual savings + current portfolio
× real return). For an exact answer, change the input in Projections, or use
`fire_status_summary` before and after, and quote the difference in retire age.

## Taxes

- Side business profit: income tax + ~15.3% self-employment tax on net
  (Schedule C). Set aside **25–30% of profit** as a starting rule and pay
  quarterly estimates once you expect to owe $1,000 or more for the year.
- Interest and non-qualified dividends are taxed as ordinary income. Qualified
  dividends and long-term gains get lower rates.
- Rental of personal property: income is taxable, and expenses may be limited.
- Planning estimates only. Confirm with a CPA.
