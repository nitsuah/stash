# Low-touch income streams

Ranges are **realistic year-one results for a solo beginner**, not best cases.
Many people earn $0 from a new shop or site for the first few months. Rates and
fees change, so check current ones before quoting.

| # | Stream | Setup | Upkeep/wk | Capital | Year-1 net/mo | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Cash yield: HYSA, CD ladder, T-bills | 1–2 h | ~0 | the cash itself | balance × yield ÷ 12 | very low (FDIC/Treasury) |
| 2 | Dividend / index portfolio | 1–2 h | ~0 | invested savings | balance × yield ÷ 12 | market risk |
| 3 | Declutter → resell on autopilot | 3–5 h | 2–3 h | $0 | $100–$600 while stock lasts | very low |
| 4 | Digital downloads on Etsy (spreadsheets, planners, templates) | 20–40 h | 1–2 h | ~$20–$50 | $0–$300 | low (time) |
| 5 | Print-on-demand (Etsy + Printify/Printful) | 15–30 h | 1–3 h | ~$20–$100 | $0–$200 | low–medium (ads) |
| 6 | Rent out gear or space (camera, tools, parking, storage) | 2–5 h | 0.5–2 h | what you own | $50–$400 | medium (damage) |
| 7 | Flipping (buy to resell) | 5 h | 5–10 h | $100–$500 | $200–$1,000 | medium (dead stock) |
| 8 | Niche content / affiliate site or newsletter | 40–80 h | 3–5 h | ~$50–$150 a year | $0–$100 | high (time, algorithms) |

## 1. Cash yield

- **Fit:** everyone, and it's the first move when runway is short: it earns on
  money you're keeping safe anyway.
- **How:** emergency fund in a HYSA; money you won't need for 3–12 months in a
  **CD ladder** (fire shows each maturity on the Projections chart) or T-bills.
- **In fire:** account with APY in Financial Overview, or CDs in the add form. The
  Cash & Fixed Income card totals yearly interest.
- **Red flags:** "guaranteed" yields well above Treasury rates, and crypto "savings".

## 2. Dividend / index portfolio

- Educational only: broad, low-cost funds beat chasing yield. A high yield is often
  a warning sign. Point to fire-coach for allocation, and to fire's Insights
  concentration tiles.

## 3. Declutter → resell on autopilot

- The best first stream: no capital, it teaches pricing and shipping, and it funds
  the next stream. Use the **reseller-autopilot** skill. The weekly routine is in
  its listing playbook.

## 4. Digital downloads on Etsy

- **Fit:** spreadsheet, design or writing skills. Sold over and over with no
  shipping, so it's the most genuinely "AFK" once built.
- **Ideas that suit FIRE people:** budget and FIRE-number spreadsheets, debt
  payoff trackers, CD ladder planners, side-hustle profit and tax trackers, and
  printable savings challenges. Never include licensed brand assets or anything
  that gives personalized financial advice.
- **First 30 days:** research 10 best-selling competitor listings and note price,
  format and review complaints → build one product better than those complaints
  → make 5 mockup images → write the listing (13 tags, keyword-first title) →
  publish 3 variants → add 1 listing a week.
- **Fees:** $0.20 listing + 6.5% transaction + 3% + $0.25 processing (US).
- **Risks:** saturated niches, refund requests, and copycats.

## 5. Print-on-demand

- Same storefront skills as #4. The supplier prints and ships. Margins are thin
  ($3–$8 a shirt), so it's volume- and design-driven. Order a sample before
  listing, use only original or properly licensed art, and avoid trademarked
  phrases (check USPTO's trademark search).

## 6. Rent what you own

- Peer-to-peer rental platforms for cameras, tools, outdoor gear, parking spots
  or storage space. Check insurance coverage, the platform's damage guarantee,
  your lease or HOA rules, and local regulations first.

## 7. Flipping

- Only after #3 is working. Buy only when expected profit clears your hourly
  floor (~$15/hr): `(sell-through × (comp median − fees − label) − cost) ÷ hours`,
  using sold comps that move within 30 days (see the reseller-autopilot listing
  playbook). Track cost basis in the fire ledger. As a second guard, cap
  inventory spend at a fixed monthly amount and never use emergency-fund money.

## 8. Content / affiliate / newsletter

- Slowest to pay. It only works on a topic they'd write about for free for a year.
  Disclose affiliate links (FTC). Don't publish mass AI-generated pages: search
  engines and platforms demote them, and it isn't honest to readers.

## Red flags (say no, and explain why)

Pay-to-join opportunities, recruiting-based income (MLM), "done-for-you" stores
or Amazon automation services, courses whose main product is selling the course,
guaranteed returns, upfront "training" fees for a job, and anything asking for
account logins, gift cards or crypto.
